EasyShape5000 Parameter Guide

Every field, its range, its default, and what it changes

Reading This Page

Parameters are grouped by the step they appear in, then by the section heading they sit under in the panel. That is the order you meet them in the app: XY Geometry before you generate, Z Strategy before the preview, Feeds & Toolpaths before the toolpaths.

Four columns are worth explaining:

Values
The range the field accepts, or the options in the dropdown. Out-of-range entries are rejected by the form rather than clamped silently.
Default
What a fresh operation starts with. Tool-derived fields have no default - they are seeded from the tool you pick.
Applies to
Which operations show the field. A field you cannot find is usually one that belongs to a different operation type.
Shown when
Several fields are conditional and only appear once another setting is on, or set to a particular value. If a parameter is documented here but missing from your panel, this is why.

The same text is in the app

Every description below is the tooltip text for that field - hover the ? beside any label to get it in place. This page and the app read from the same string pack, so they cannot drift apart.

For what each operation does, see the Operation Guide. For how the stages fit together, see How a Job Works.

Machine settings are not on this page

Safe Z, travel Z, stock thickness, Z-zero reference, post-processor, line numbers and tool length compensation are job-wide rather than per-operation. They are covered in the Workflow Guide.

Workholding & Ends

ParameterValuesDefaultApplies toWhat it does
Axis Over-Cut
Shown when Rotary Kinematics is Indexed 3+1 (flat faces at fixed angles)		 0 - 10 mm 0 Rotary / 4th Axis How far past the rotation axis each face may cut. Opposing faces that stop exactly on the axis are tangent, not overlapping, so backlash or a touch-off error leaves a spine on the centreline. Leave at 0 for one tool radius automatically. This deepens the waste only - it never cuts into the minimum-radius drive stub, so you can set both.
Minimum Radius 0 - 50 mm 2 Rotary / 4th Axis Radius of the drive stub the tool never cuts into: what a chuck grips and a tailstock centre bears on. It is also the radius a Taper end cones down to. On Indexed 3+1 it applies as a cylinder about the centreline, so a value here is a hard floor on how deep any face can go near the axis - the default 2mm keeps a 2mm core no face will cut through. Set it to 0 for a free end you want to part right through.
Workholding Between Centers (tailstock supported) / Chuck Only (cantilevered) Between Centers (tailstock supported) Rotary / 4th Axis Between Centers assumes a tailstock supports the far end, so material past that end is protected. Chuck Only assumes the part is cantilevered and the far end is open air.
Chuck-End Boundary Stop at geometry (turned to the core) / Roll Over - lip (needs a flat end) / Taper - cone down to the drive stub Stop at geometry (turned to the core) Rotary / 4th Axis What happens where the model ends at the chuck. Stop at geometry is the default and the reliable choice: the waste is turned down to the minimum radius and the tool stops at the part. Roll Over leaves a small lip around the edge, but only works on an end that is flat and close to square against the axis - a slanted, domed or off-centre end will show a scalloped ring, and you will get a warning. Taper cones down to the drive stub instead of stepping.
Chuck-End Offset -25 - 60 mm 0 Rotary / 4th Axis Moves where the chuck end of the model is treated as ending, along the axis. Negative stops short of the real edge - use it for a slanted base or a tangent slope your tool cannot enter cleanly. Positive machines further past it, turning the waste down to the minimum radius as it goes. Too large a negative value leaves nothing to machine and you will get a warning. On Roll Over and Taper this is the lip depth or cone length instead, and is always positive.
Tail-End Boundary Stop at geometry (turned to the core) / Roll Over - lip (needs a flat end) / Taper - cone down to the drive stub Stop at geometry (turned to the core) Rotary / 4th Axis What happens where the model ends at the tailstock. Roll Over leaves a small lip around the edge, but only works on an end that is flat and close to square against the axis - a slanted, domed or off-centre end will show a scalloped ring, and you will get a warning. Taper cones down to the drive stub instead of stepping.
Tail-End Offset -25 - 60 mm 0 Rotary / 4th Axis Moves where the tailstock end of the model is treated as ending, along the axis. Negative stops short of the real edge - use it for a slanted top or a tangent slope your tool cannot enter cleanly. Positive machines further past it, turning the waste down to the minimum radius as it goes. Too large a negative value leaves nothing to machine and you will get a warning.

EasyShape5000 Operation Guide

What each EasyShape5000 operation does, and what's coming

Overview

EasyShape assigns operations per shape. Select one or more shapes in the scene tree or on the canvas, drop them into an operation bucket, set parameters, and generate geometry. Operations are independent and fully undoable. All shapes must be closed paths - open paths trigger a warning. Every parameter named here is documented on the Parameter Reference.

Seven operations produce toolpaths today. The 3D set (V-Carve, Relief, Rotary) is newer than the rest and under active testing - dry-run before you cut. Only the Pattern Generator is still scaffolding.

Profile Cut Stable

Cuts along a shape's contour. Cut side chooses outside (part stays full size), inside (cut within the boundary), or on-path (tool centered on the line). Each shape produces a single offset pass; holding tabs can leave bridges so parts don't break free on the last depth pass.

Use it for: cutting parts out, interior windows, and centerline scoring.

Key parameters: cut side; detect nested holes; tabs (count, width, height); depth and entry settings on the strategy stage.

Pocket Clearing Stable

Clears the entire interior of a shape to a set depth using concentric inward offsets spaced by step-over. Shapes nested inside the boundary are treated as islands (holes) and left standing. A raster/zig-zag strategy is planned but not yet implemented.

Use it for: recesses, inlay cavities, and surfacing bounded regions.

Key parameters: step-over; clearing strategy (concentric offsets); optional finishing pass + finish step-over; depth and entry settings.

Drilling Stable

Treats circles and obround slots as holes and selects peck/plunge or circular milling automatically, depending on hole size versus tool. Uses the same engine as EasyTrace; the only difference is that shapes arrive one at a time rather than as a batch file.

Use it for: mounting holes, dowel holes, and slots.

Key parameters: mill oversize holes + mill step-over; canned cycle, peck depth, dwell, retract height; optional spot drill (+ spot depth).

Engraving Stable

Traces a path directly with no offset — the tool centre follows the line. Bypasses the offset pipeline and Clipper entirely, so open paths are as valid as closed ones and nothing collapses when the tool is wider than the shape.

Use it for: lettering, outlines, score lines, and any single-line artwork.

Key parameters: cut depth; multi-depth + depth per pass; feeds and speeds.

3D Operations Beta

The three operations below produce per-point Z toolpaths rather than flat contours. They run on a background worker, report progress in stages, and render in the 3D preview (F2) with playback. They are the newest part of the suite — verify output before cutting.

V-Carve Beta

Variable-depth carving with a V-bit. The engine samples each closed region's boundary, builds a Voronoi/Delaunay medial axis, and drops the bit until its flanks touch both walls — so cut width tracks the shape's local thickness automatically. Regions wider than the bit can reach at max depth get a flat floor with a stitched perimeter.

Use it for: carved lettering, decorative inlays, and sign work.

Key parameters: V-bit included angle and tip diameter; start depth; max depth; chain/corner quality settings.

For a V-bit, tool diameter means the tip flat, not the cutting diameter at the surface. A 90° bit with a 0.3 mm tip is entered as 0.3.

3D Relief Beta

Surfaces an imported STL as a height field. The mesh is sliced into a heightmap at generation time (resolution, orientation and Z datum are operation parameters, not import settings), the target surface is compensated for the real tool shape, and the result is rastered into roughing layers plus a finishing pass.

Use it for: moulds, terrain tiles, medallions, and any 2.5D surface from a mesh.

Key parameters: tool shape and corner radius; depth mapping and total depth; boundary mode; rough/finish mode, stepdown, stepover and stock-to-leave; raster axis and cell size.

Rotary - 4th Axis Beta

One operation type with two engines, chosen by the kinematics parameter.

Radial (continuous)
The mesh is sliced into a cylindrical radius map R(x, θ) and machined as a developed strip that the post converts into A-axis motion. Best for turned, wrapped or fully round parts.
Indexed 3+1
The blank is treated as an N-sided prism: the part is machined as N ordinary flat reliefs at fixed A angles, joined by pure positioning moves. Best for undercuts and features a continuous wrap can't reach.

Use it for: table legs, chess pieces, columns, and faceted or multi-sided parts.

Key parameters: kinematics; rotary axis and model orientation; stock shape and blank diameter (or face count and blank width, indexed); workholding mode with per-end settings; minimum radius (drive core); rough/finish stepdown and stepover.

Rotary output is generated in the machine frame: workspace rotation, mirroring and origin do not apply to it, and the max-safe-depth guard is a flat-stock rule that a surface-datum rotary job legitimately passes. Check your A-axis route and travel limits on the controller first.
